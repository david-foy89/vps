A standing pilot is a second burner with its own gas path, its own orifice, and its own way of going out. It exists to light the main burner. On a lot of firetube equipment it also exists because that is how the heater was built twenty years ago. The fuel it burns is not sales gas. The fittings it adds are more places to leak.

A pilotless burner lights the main flame directly. SureFire’s pilotless FT units, available from VPS, delete the pilot burner and the pilot fuel train. The size range operators actually buy runs from a 1-inch mixer at 125,000 BTU/hr (FT-2) to a 4-inch mixer at 1,500,000 BTU/hr (FT-8), with 2-inch and 3-inch sizes in between. Nozzles for horizontal or vertical firetubes start at the FT-4.

## What you gain, in plain terms

Fewer parts and fewer joints. Less fuel spent keeping a pilot alive. A lower bill up front, because you are not buying a pilot train, and a lower bill later, because you are not maintaining one. Those are the reasons SureFire built the pilotless line, and they are the reasons a production foreman usually cares.

There is an emissions side to the same hardware. Gas that is not continuously fed to a pilot is gas that is not burned or vented for no process reason. Paired with a ventless fuel train, that is part of how these systems are designed to lower methane. It is still not a compliance certificate. Your environmental lead has to map the package to the rule that applies to the site.

## When a pilot should stay

Not every flame should be pilotless. Combustors and flares are usually a piloted, flame-front problem, handled by the FTL-F units and often a BMS-100, not by a firetube nozzle. Some firetubes will stay piloted because of the vessel, the gas, or a preference not to rebuild the fuel train yet. That is what the FT-1 and the SF-50 are for: a proper pilot, supervised, instead of a manual relight.

If you are unsure which side of that line you are on, describe the equipment and whether you need temperature control. VPS will say if the honest scope is a pilotless FT unit, a pilot maintainer, or a full heater controller.
