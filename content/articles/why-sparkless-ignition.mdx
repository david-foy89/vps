Spark ignition is simple until the site is not. A gap has to stay clean. A coil has to stay dry. Production gas does not cooperate. Moisture, dirt, and vibration are normal on a heater treater or a combustor, and they are hard on the parts that make a spark.

SureFire’s patented sparkless ignition, supplied in this territory by Vista Process Solutions, uses a hot surface instead. There is no gap to set and no ignition coil in the circuit. The igniter is built to live in that environment longer, and the maintenance list gets shorter because two familiar failure points are gone.

## Safer does not mean unattended forever

Hot-surface ignition is safer than spark ignition for a specific reason: it removes hardware that fails in predictable, annoying ways, and it is paired with flame proving. Safer is not the same claim as “this burner cannot go out.” A proved flame that is lost should still close the fuel. That shutdown is the safety function. The ignition method is how you get the flame in the first place, and how often you have to go back to get it again.

Field crews feel the difference in callouts. A coil or a fouled plug becomes a trip. A design that does not use either one spends that time somewhere else. SureFire presents this as a longevity and maintenance-cost choice, not as a gadget. That is the right way to judge it: count the parts you no longer stock, and count the relights you no longer make.

## Where it shows up

The FT ignition series uses this hot-surface approach on firetube equipment, from a pilot-sized FT-1 up through pilotless burners. The largest, the FT-8, is rated to 1,500,000 BTU/hr on a 4-inch mixer. Flare and combustor pilots in the FTL-F family use the same ignition idea in a flame-front assembly, because a stack is not a firetube.
