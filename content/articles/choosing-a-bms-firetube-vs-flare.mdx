Firetubes and flares both burn gas. They do not want the same controller. Picking from the wrong family wastes a quote cycle and, if it gets installed, leaves the site with a unit that cannot see the thing you care about — temperature on a treater, or a pilot on a stack.

## Firetube equipment

Heater treaters, dehydrators, line heaters, and similar process heaters are firetube work. The usual SureFire pairing, supplied by VPS, is a BMS-300 or BMS-350 plus an FT ignition unit.

Choose the controller from the temperature job. The BMS-300 reads an RTD and can run the fuel train with or without a pilot. The BMS-350 is the one to ask for when you have more than one temperature input, and it can prove flame with a thermocouple or a flame rod. Both take a standby or shutdown input from the rest of the skid.

Choose the igniter from the mixer. One inch and 125,000 BTU/hr is the FT-2. Two inch and 500,000 is the FT-4. Three inch and 1,000,000 is the FT-6. Four inch and 1,500,000 is the FT-8. If the firetube is keeping a separate pilot, look at the FT-1 instead of a pilotless body.

If you do not need temperature control and only need the pilot to stay lit, stop before you buy a full BMS. The SF-50 is the automated pilot maintainer for firetubes and for pit flares.

## Flares and combustors

Stacks and combustors belong with flame-front pilots. The BMS-100 supervises a continuous pilot and a second stage. Ignition hardware is the FTL-F line: shorter assemblies for combustors and enclosed flares, and 5-, 7-, or 17-foot pilots for flare service. Inlet size on those pilots is 1/4-inch NPT, not the large firetube mixer connection.

A pit flare with gas that is not always there is closer to the SF-50 than to a heater controller. Say if the gas is intermittent. It changes the recommendation.

## What to have in hand

You do not need a full datasheet to start. VPS can sort the family from five facts: vessel type, piloted or pilotless today, mixer size or pilot length, fuel-gas trouble if you have it, and whether the site is in Texas, Oklahoma, Louisiana, or New Mexico. Southern New Mexico is covered. Northern New Mexico is not. If you are near the line, ask before the order is written.
